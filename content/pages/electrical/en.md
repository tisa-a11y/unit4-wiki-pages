# Electrical

## Concept
A new Eaton 125-amp panel goes in the dining-room wall (D04), centered in the upper-left moulding frame. It feeds the kitchen, the bathroom, the attic and the room outlets.

## Panel, feeder and circuits
- **Panel:** the Eaton box (BRP20L125G, 20 spaces) is on site. NEXT HVAC installs the panel and brings the feeder in from outside in 1" metal conduit, with a permit. The feeder size is not settled (80 amps on #4 wire, or 100 amps); Serge and Alex confirm it Monday, Nov 23. Do not close the panel wall until the cable entry from the side is worked out.
- **Kitchen — nine circuits (Romex, arc-fault and ground-fault protected):** three 20-amp counter circuits, a 20-amp refrigerator circuit, dishwasher, disposal, range ignition, the hood's own 15-amp circuit, and lights.
- **Bathroom and attic:** two 20-amp GFCI outlet boxes in the bathroom; the bathroom ceiling wire runs straight to a junction box in the attic; an attic light with a switch; and a 120V GFCI outlet within 25 ft that stays live when the furnace is off.
- **Rooms with closed walls (bedroom, living, dining, hallway):** set outlet boxes and run empty flexible conduit (smurf tube). No wire is pulled yet. No outlet on the door-swing wall by the panel. Stay out of the pocket-door cavities. Run a coiled low-voltage switch cable up the left wall of the bedroom closet to the attic.

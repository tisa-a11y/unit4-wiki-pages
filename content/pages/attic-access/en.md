# Attic Access

## Concept
A permanent hatch in the hallway ceiling, 24" × 36" clear when finished, sealed and insulated. In the attic: a 24"-wide wood walkway, a level 30" × 30" platform at the furnace, a light with a switch, and a live outlet.

## Five rules before any cutting
1. **Check the ceiling first.** Find out whether this ceiling needs a fire-rated hatch, and measure the width between the joists (the plan assumes a 27-1/2" wood cut). Do this before buying wood or cutting.
2. **Seal and insulate the hatch.** Backer rod (3/8") and sealant on the fixed frame. Foam weatherstrip (1/2" × 1/8") on the 1×2 ledges. Do not caulk the lid seam; the lid has to lift out. Two fiberglass batts that match the attic insulation sit on the lid, held by three loose 16-gauge wire loops on screw eyes no longer than 1/2". Build a plywood dam on all four sides, 2" higher than the loose insulation.
3. **Keep the opening clear.** No latch or hardware may stick into the 24" × 36" opening. The recessed lid finish is waiting for Tisa's OK.
4. **Leave the bedroom closet ceiling open** until the low-voltage switch cable is in and the permanent hatch exists.
5. **Fix leaks first.** Nothing gets closed in or insulated while the roof or attic leaks.

## Walkway, platform and power
- **Walkway:** 24" wide with 30" of clear passage, no longer than 20 ft (the attic is 66" high). Use 23/32" rated OSB, 3/4" plywood or 2× lumber. Never 3/8". One strip per 4×8 sheet. Screw into the framing with #9 wood screws, 2-1/2" or 3" long. (Code: CMC 304.4.)
- **Platform:** level, at least 30" × 30", screwed to the framing on the side where the furnace is serviced. No vibration pads needed from John. Keep 1" clear of the Type B vent. (Code: CMC 304.4.3.)
- **Power:** an enclosed LED light with a switch at the hatch, and a 120V GFCI outlet within 25 ft that stays live when the furnace is switched off.
